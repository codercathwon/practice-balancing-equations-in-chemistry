    Tips

    How to balance an equation
    1. Write down how many of each atom are on the left and on the right (the atom counter does this for you).
    2. Pick an element that appears in only one substance on each side, and balance it first.
    3. Change only the coefficients (the big numbers in front). Never change the small subscripts — that would make a different substance.
    4. Balance O and H last, since they usually show up in many places.
    5. Recount everything. If all coefficients share a factor (like 2, 4, 6), divide them down to the smallest whole numbers.
    Tricks that save time
    • Polyatomic ions as one unit. If SO₄, NO₃, PO₄ or OH appears unchanged on both sides, count it as one block instead of counting S and O separately.
    • Fractions are fine while you work. If you need 3½ O₂, write it as 7/2 in your head, then double every coefficient.
    • Combustion order: C → H → O. Carbon goes to CO₂, hydrogen goes to H₂O, and O₂ is fixed last.
    • Odd–even trick. If O comes as O₂ on one side and you have an odd number of O on the other, put a 2 in front of the odd one first.
    • Free elements go last. Substances made of one element (Fe, O₂, Cl₂) are the easiest to adjust, because changing them affects only that element.
    • Diatomic elements: H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂ always come in pairs when they're on their own.
    Common mistakes
    • Changing a subscript (H₂O → H₂O₂) to make the numbers work. That's hydrogen peroxide, not water.
    • Forgetting that a coefficient multiplies every atom in the formula, including the ones inside parentheses: 3 Ca(OH)₂ has 3 Ca, 6 O and 6 H.
    • Stopping before checking every element once more at the end.
    • Leaving the answer as 2, 4, 2 when 1, 2, 1 works. Always use the smallest whole numbers.
    Worked example: propane burning

    C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O

    1. C: 3 on the left, so put 3 in front of CO₂.
    2. H: 8 on the left, so put 4 in front of H₂O (4 × 2 = 8).
    3. O: the right now has 3 × 2 + 4 × 1 = 10 O, so put 5 in front of O₂.
    4. Check: C 3 = 3, H 8 = 8, O 10 = 10.

    C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O
